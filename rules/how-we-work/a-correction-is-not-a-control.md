# A correction is not a control unless something can fail it

**Task type:** agent workflow — any long task where you are correcting your own work as you go.
**Related:** [`self-validation-loop-after-generating`](self-validation-loop-after-generating.md) tells you to
check your output; this is what to do when the checking becomes the output.
[`summaries-must-carry-the-whole-set`](../analytics/summaries-must-carry-the-whole-set.md) is one of the
defects the apparatus in §3 reliably starts committing.

---

## The rule

**Count your corrections against your changes of direction. If the ratio is high, the corrections have become
decoration.**

Retracting your own claims feels like the opposite of motivated reasoning, which is exactly why it is such a
comfortable place to end up. Each retraction is real, costly, and evidence of rigour taken alone. But a
retraction that the object absorbs and survives unchanged is **an output, not a control** — and from the
inside, a stream of them is indistinguishable from actually being careful.

**The diagnostic is one line: how many times did you correct something, and how many times did a correction
end an object rather than amend it?** A control has an off-switch. If none of yours has ever thrown it, you do
not have controls, you have commentary.

## The pattern in full

In the session this comes from: **twelve retractions, zero changes of direction.** Every one was absorbed by
the same design, which was the same shape at the end as it had been nine review rounds earlier. Several of the
corrections genuinely cost their author — reversing a favourable finding, conceding a wrong method, restoring
a disposition they had argued against. The volume was treated, by that author, as evidence the process was
working.

The subtler move underneath it: when the corrections finally reached something structural, the object was
**reclassified rather than abandoned** — relabelled into a weaker category that an existing rule already
permitted. **Reclassifying and continuing is not a correction. It is a re-permission**, and it is what the
correction habit will reach for when the alternative is stopping.

## What to do

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name the abandon condition before round 2** | *What would a correction have to reveal for me to drop this rather than fix it?* If you cannot answer, the object is being defended, not reviewed |
| **Track corrections against direction changes** | A high ratio is the signal. It is cheap to count and nobody counts it |
| **Distrust the reclassification** | If a correction ends with the thing surviving in a weaker category, check whether that category is one you already had permission for. That is the exit motivated reasoning takes when it can no longer argue the original claim |
| **Hand the resolution away** | When the reading that saves your work is available and plausible, you cannot tell it from motivated reasoning — and neither can anyone reading you. State the conflict, let someone with no stake resolve it |

## §3 — Audit the apparatus itself. It decays fastest.

The instruments built to catch drift — the index of what supersedes what, the changelog, the lessons file, the
cost summary — are **the least-audited artifacts in a project**, because everyone points them at other work.

In the same session, all four had failed:

- the **index** built to prevent citing stale figures was itself citing three superseded ones;
- the **cost document** asking a principal to set a budget contained a **false claim about its own dataset**
  (*"never over-estimated"* when two of fourteen were);
- the **lessons file** gave **three different counts for one set of lessons**;
- and the **lesson about misstating a probability misstated that probability in its own worked example.**

> **Every instrument that checks other work needs a scheduled pass in which it is the subject.** Not because
> its authors are careless — because nobody is assigned to it, and the failure is invisible from inside the
> instrument.

## Guard

Before adding a correction to a document you have already corrected several times, ask: **what would have to
be true for me to delete this document instead?** If nothing would, you are not reviewing it. You are keeping
it alive one amendment at a time.
